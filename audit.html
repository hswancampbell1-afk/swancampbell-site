What arrives

An ordinary Tuesday.

The figure above is the easy half. The harder question is what actually lands, and when. This is a constructed day — not a client's, and not a good day chosen carefully — but every item on it is something the service produces, in the form it produces it.

  1. 07:40
    Forty-one messages arrived overnight, sorted before you open them. Nineteen newsletters and three circulars filed and out of the way. Nine for information. Seven that expect an answer, each with a draft already waiting. And three marked as today's, left undrafted on purpose — those need you, not a draft of you.
  2. 07:41
    One of the seven, waiting in your own Drafts folder:
    From Marion, 06:58

    Can I have the board pack before Thursday? I’d like to read it on Wednesday evening.

    Re: Board pack · draft, not sent

    Hi Marion, you’ll have the board pack by [confirm day and time]. [Anything worth flagging before you read it on Wednesday.]

    Everything outside the brackets is from Marion’s email. The date is yours to give, and so is anything worth flagging; the software will not guess either in your name.

  3. 11:30
    Yesterday's call written up. Three decisions, six actions with an owner and a date on each, two open questions nobody answered — and a follow-up email drafted underneath it, ready to go out in your name once you've read it.
  4. 14:10
    Two receipts landed and went straight into the spreadsheet — a £53.20 rail fare, recorded zero-rated, and a £276.00 software renewal with the £46.00 of VAT split out. A third was flagged rather than entered: a photographed restaurant bill where the VAT line was unreadable. It's in the review queue with the photo attached, not guessed at.
  5. 16:45
    An invoice thirty-two days overdue, with a reminder drafted in the wording you approved. And tomorrow's 9am is flagged: it sits twenty minutes after a train gets in, which is the conflict check rather than something you were going to notice on the platform.
  6. 18:00
    Ninety-two messages by the end of the day, and twenty drafts written. None of them was sent. Everything above is sitting in your drafts, waiting for you.

That Tuesday is Back Office, not every package. Inbox Starter is the triage, the invoice chaser and the Monday round-up. The drafts, the write-up, the receipts and the conflict check are what Back Office adds. A research brief is not a standing item in the afternoon: on Back Office+ it is one of six on-request jobs a month, and extras are £15.

Replies stay in those drafts until you send them. The only message the system can send is an overdue-invoice reminder, and only after you switch that on in writing and approve the template. That is the guarantee.
