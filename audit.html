What arrives

An ordinary Tuesday.

The figure above is the easy half. The harder question is what actually lands, and when. This is a constructed day — not a client's, and not a good day chosen carefully — but every item on it is something the service produces, in the form it produces it.

  1. 07:40
    Overnight mail sorted before you open it. Nine messages: four filed as reading, three drafted, one marked urgent, one left alone because it needed you and not a draft of you.
  2. 07:41
    One of the three, waiting in your own Drafts folder:
    Re: Thursday's board pack — draft, not sent Hi Marion — Thursday still works. I'll have the pack with you by Wednesday lunchtime so there's a clear evening to read it. One thing worth flagging in advance: the Q3 headcount line has moved since we last spoke, and I'd rather walk you through it than have you find it on the night.
  3. 11:30
    Yesterday's call written up. Three decisions, five actions with an owner and a date on each, two open questions nobody answered — and a follow-up email drafted underneath it, ready to go out in your name once you've read it.
  4. 14:10
    Two receipts landed and went straight into the spreadsheet — a £38.40 train fare and a £212.00 software renewal, both with the VAT split out. A third was flagged rather than entered: a photographed restaurant bill where the VAT line was unreadable. It's in the review queue with the photo attached, not guessed at.
  5. 16:45
    One page on tomorrow's 9am. Who the company is, who's actually on the call, what they announced last quarter, and the two things that have changed since you last met them — with a source next to every claim, so you can check the one that matters before you repeat it.
  6. 18:00
    Nothing was sent. Everything above is sitting in your drafts, waiting for you.

That last line is the whole arrangement. The guarantee explains why it isn't just a promise.
