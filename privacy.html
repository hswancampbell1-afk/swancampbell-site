Privacy

Harrison Swan-Campbell, trading as Swan Campbell · Last updated 8 September 2026

Two different things happen here, and they deserve separate answers. Visiting this website collects nothing at all. Becoming a client means I handle your business mail, and that is governed by a written agreement you sign before anything is connected. Both are set out below.

1This website collects nothing

No analytics. No tracking pixels. No advertising tags. No third-party scripts of any kind. No cookies — which is why there is no cookie banner asking you to accept some.

There is no contact form. The only way to reach me from this page is an email address, and following it opens your own mail programme. Nothing is submitted to me until you choose to send it.

The page loads two typefaces from Google Fonts, which means Google receives your IP address in order to serve them. That is the single third party involved in viewing this site, and it is mentioned because a privacy notice that says “nothing at all” while quietly making a request to another company is not being straight with you.

2If you email me

I hold what you send: your name, your email address, and whatever you write. I use it to reply and, if we go on to work together, to set you up. The lawful basis is my legitimate interest in answering people who contact me about my services, and, once we are talking about an agreement, taking steps at your request before entering into one.

Enquiries that do not become clients are deleted within twelve months. I do not add you to a mailing list, and I do not have one.

3If you become a client

Here you are the controller and I am the processor. In plain terms: it stays your data, I only do what you have instructed, and your instructions are the written agreement you sign. This section summarises clause 8 of that agreement, which is the document that actually governs it.

What I handle
The content and metadata of your business email, your calendar entries, and the invoices and receipts you give me.
Whose data that includes
You, your staff, your clients, and anyone who corresponds with you.
What I do with it
Sort your mail, draft replies for you to approve, write up meetings, record receipts, and prepare briefs for meetings you are about to have.
How long
For the term of the agreement. Within 30 days of it ending I return anything of yours I hold in a usable format and delete my copies.

4Who else sees it

Three others, and no more without telling you first:

  • Anthropic PBC (Claude API, United States) classifies mail and drafts text. Your data is not used to train their models. Because this involves sending data outside the UK, that transfer is covered by a data processing agreement incorporating the UK international data transfer addendum.
  • Microsoft or Google — whoever already runs your mail. They continue to hold it exactly as they did before; I connect to your existing account rather than moving anything.
  • ntfy sends me a push notification when something in your inbox looks urgent. That alert carries your firm’s name and a count of messages — never a subject line, a sender, or any part of the correspondence. It tells me to look; it does not tell me what at.

I will tell you in writing at least 14 days before adding any other, and you may object. Nobody else has access to your data: I do not engage a substitute, and there is no team.

Meeting briefs search the open web. What leaves is the name of the company or person I am briefing you on — never your mail, your calendar or your documents. If there is anyone you would rather I did not look up, tell me and I will not, for a single meeting or as a standing instruction.

5How it is kept

  • Credentials are encrypted at rest, and the machine they sit on is encrypted.
  • The access you grant is limited to reading and drafting. On Microsoft 365 the permission does not include sending at all.
  • Anything the system is unsure of is held for me to look at rather than acted on.
  • Bank details, card numbers and similar are masked on my screen when I review your mail. I do not need to see them to do this work, so I do not.
  • You can withdraw my access at any time from your own account settings, without asking me. It takes effect immediately and my stored credentials for you are deleted.

If there is ever a personal data breach affecting your data, I will tell you without undue delay and within 48 hours.

6Your rights

Under UK data protection law you can ask for a copy of the personal data I hold about you, ask me to correct it, ask me to delete it, object to how I am using it, or ask me to restrict it. Ask me and I will answer within one month.

Where I am acting as a processor for a client — that is, if you are one of their customers or correspondents rather than my client — the right way round is to ask them, and I will help them answer you.

If you think I have got something wrong, please tell me first so I can put it right. You also have the right to complain to the Information Commissioner’s Office at ico.org.uk, or by calling 0303 123 1113.
