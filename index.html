Constructed, not a client’s, but this time it is exactly what the software writes. Everything outside the brackets came from Tom’s own email. Everything inside them is something only you know, and it will never guess those in your name: not the date, not the reason, and never whether the numbers are fine. Filling three brackets takes seconds. The email around them was the part that took the time.

The guarantee

Nothing goes out in your name without you approving it.

Replies are drafted, never sent. Calendar invitations go only when you press the button. Invoice reminders are drafts too by default; if you'd rather they went out automatically, you switch that on and approve the wording first. That isn't a promise about how I work — it's built into the software I use.

Drafts appear in your own Drafts folder as mail arrives, so you may see one before I have. I review every one within one business day; my review improves them, and you decide what gets sent.

On Microsoft 365, sending permission is only requested if you switch on automatic invoice reminders. Until then, the consent screen does not include sending. If you later switch that on, you'll be asked to reconnect so Microsoft can grant it. Google's nearest equivalent is broader and does allow sending, so there the guarantee rests on the software alone. I'd rather tell you which is which than let you assume they're the same.
